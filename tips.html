General Tips

  • Pay attention to the gym's rules
  • Be aware of people around you, especially when walking near the walls or landing areas.
  • Expect to fall and fail often, it's a normal process.
  • Don't compare your progress with other climbers. Go at your own pace.
  • Wear comfortable clothing that allows you to be flexible.
  • Ask people around you if you are unsure about something
  • Remember to take breaks when you need to
  • Stop if your hands are becoming too tired and blisters appear

Tips to help you improve

  • Film yourself climb

    A way to improve your climbing is seeing yourself climb from a different perspective. You might be able to get to the root of your problem from seeing yourself climb from afar

  • Clean your shoes from chalk

    There is a lot of chalk around the gym, after a while your shoes will be completely white. Chalk makes your fingers sticky, but not for your shoes. Only time you should let chalk on your shoes is if your shoes are wet. Try to keep it chalk free.

  • Don't over-grip or re-grip too much

    A frequent mistake beginners make is when they grab a hold they adjust their hands way too much. It's better to grab it once, maybe adjust it one time and then move on.

  • Climbing faster

    Beginners spend too much time on the wall and get tired way faster. Climbing fast removes that issue. You should first visualize how to get from point A to point B and try to climb it as fast as you can. It might not be the "perfect technique" but the difficulty level will drastically decrease if you implement that.

  • Climb with straight arms

    Beginners bend their arms since they don't know how to control their power on the wall, with bending your arms it will be a lot harder to climb since the tension is a lot greater for your muscles. Keeping a straight arm will help your stamina immensely.

  • Wave your arms

    It's important to keep your hands as dry as possible, a lot of climbers wave their arms around when they climb. Waving your arms around will help you get as much air as possible and keep your hands dry.

Training & Diet

Training

  • Dynamic Climbing

    What it does:

    • Improve dynamic movement:

      • How to:

        0 - 20 degree overhang

        3 boulders around V0

        Move without stopping

        Rest 2-5 minutes between each boulder

  • No Hands Slab

    What it does:

    • Climbing without the use of your hands, Improves performance of footwork.

      • How to:

        Find inclined wall

        Climb the wall with minimal assistance from hands

        Rest 1-2 minutes between each boulder

        Repeat 6 times

  • Pull-up Negatives

    • How to:

      Jump to the top of the bar

      Slowly lower yourself

      Repeat 5 times per set

      3 sets in total

      2 minutes rest per set

      use resistance band if necessary

  • Basic Grip Engagement

    3 basic grip types to maximize

    • half crimp
    • open-handed grip
    • sloper

    instead of practicing, you need to memorize, and learn how to hold correctly.

    How to:

    Perform them on a hangboard.

    Don't let your feet leave the ground

    Set 1: Half crimp (fingers are 90 degree angle)
    • 5 seconds on, 5 seconds off
    • Repeat 5 times
    • NOTE: Keep a VERY low intensity, no more than a light strain
    Set 2: Open hand 3 finger drag
    • 5 seconds on, 5 seconds off
    • Repeat 5 times
    • NOTE: Keep a VERY low intensity, no more than a light strain
    Set 3: Sloper (rounded hold)
    • 5 seconds on, 5 seconds off
    • Repeat 5 times
    • NOTE: Keep a VERY low intensity, no more than a light strain

Diet

Protein

Aim for 3 main meals with protein, built up to 4-5 meals/day if you are training hard

Portion size: about a palm-sized amount per meal (~25/30g protein)

A whole chicken breast is roughly around 45g.

  • Morning protein: starting the day with a bigger protein meal tends to help, protein is satiating, so a low-protein breakfast (e.g. toast + peanut butter, granola + oat milk) often leads to snacking later

Carbohydrates

roughly 150g/day on non-training days.

on training days double your baseline → 300g

before/during: 30-60 g, then possibly another 30/60g+ during a longer session.

go for fast-digesting options: bananas, muesli/granola bar, fruit juice

Caffeine

Caffeine helps power output, focus, alertness and lowers perceived exertion

However caffeine can mask under-fueling. It feels good acutely but poor for long-term recovery.

Instead of caffeine, eating cookies/brownies/chocolate before a session is a better use of energy, since it won't disrupt your recovery sleep (don't overeat processed sugary products)

Hydration

pint of water first thing in the morning
